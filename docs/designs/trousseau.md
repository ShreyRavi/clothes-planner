# Design: Trousseau, a no-login outfit planner for multi-day events

Source of truth for scope: the Trousseau PRD (Oct 5, 2026), to be committed as `docs/PRD.md`. Design source: `design/Trousseau.dc.html` (working prototype) and `design/Trousseau Handoff.dc.html` (tokens and component inventory). This doc records only the decisions made on top of the PRD.

## Problem Statement

A guest at a multi-day event, prototypically a five-function Indian wedding, needs 4 to 8 complete outfits of 6 to 12 parts each. Today the plan lives in screenshots, WhatsApp threads, and Pinterest boards. Pieces go missing (blouse stitching, dupatta, lawn-safe shoes), nobody sees all functions side by side, and sending the plan to a mother, tailor, or stylist means forwarding dozens of unstructured screenshots.

## What Makes This Cool

The app makes a better screenshot than the one you would take yourself. One tap renders a clean, labeled card per function (picked pieces, slot names, gaps) as an image, with your local photos included, ready for the phone share sheet. The person on the other end of WhatsApp sees the outfit inline, with no link to open and nothing to install.

## Constraints

- Static site only. No backend, no accounts, no server functions, no third-party data services (PRD hard constraint).
- All plan data and photos in IndexedDB; localStorage for small settings only.
- Share links encode the plan in the URL fragment and never include local photos.
- Phone first at 375 px; desktop is the same layout with more room.
- No em dashes or en dashes in user-facing copy.
- App shell under 200 KB compressed; first load under 2 s on a mid-range phone on 4G.
- Open source: must be cheap to host, easy to fork, and easy to contribute templates to.

## Landscape

Observations from a web search on 2026-10-05. These are directional, not exhaustive.

- No dedicated multi-day event outfit planner turned up. Indian wedding apps cover vendors, budgets, and guests ([PlanMyShaadi](https://peerlist.io/sidhanshu/project/planmyshaadi--ai-wedding-planner), [Meragi](https://apps.apple.com/tt/app/meragi/id6445975028)); a survey of the space found no single dedicated wedding outfit planner in India ([Glance](https://glance-corp-website.glance.inmobi.com/us/blogs/glanceai/tech/wedding-outfit-planner-app-india)); outfit "generators" are editorial lookbooks ([The Rodinhoods](https://www.therodinhoods.com/post/indian-wedding-outfit-generator)).
- The closest open-source tools catalog clothes you already own and need a self-hosted server ([Libre Closet](https://dungeontools.duckdns.org/about), [Wardrowbe](https://apps.apple.com/app/wardrowbe/id6759947671)).
- Builders who encode app state in URLs report links too long to be usable in chat apps and reach for shorteners ([Glide community](https://community.glideapps.com/t/share-link-to-current-screen-links-are-too-long/35968)). This is unverified for WhatsApp specifically, which is why the Phase 2 gate tests WhatsApp on real devices.

## Premises (agreed)

1. The tool must beat a screenshot twice: capture faster than taking a screenshot, and sharing better than forwarding a pile of them.
2. No backend and no accounts in v1 is a hard rule. It keeps the project free to host, private, and forkable.
3. Most captured images will be local photos or screenshots, not remote URLs, because pasted product links get no preview image without a server. The share link alone would therefore show mostly fallback tiles. A share image rendered on the device is added as a P0 share path; the backup file is for restore, not for sharing.
4. The design covers 7 of the 11 briefed screens. Inbox, Share sheet, Shared read-only view, and Settings are missing and must be specified before the work that needs them starts.
5. Distribution is a public deployed URL plus a public GitHub repo under a permissive license. Templates are plain data files so contributors can add traditions and event types.

## Approaches Considered

- **A: Port the prototype** (Vite + Preact, grow `Trousseau.dc.html`). Rejected because the prototype is a single-file demo in a design-tool runtime (`support.js`, template bindings) with no module boundaries or test harness, so porting it means a rewrite anyway. React was kept over Preact for contributor familiarity; React 19 plus React DOM is roughly 60 KB compressed, which fits the 200 KB shell budget. If the bundle-size check in CI fails, swapping in `preact/compat` is the fallback.
- **B: PRD stack in PRD order.** Sound foundation, but the first release cannot yet send a good-looking plan to anyone.
- **C: B's foundation, show-Mom-first order.** Chosen.

Shipping Phase 1 without backup would mean real plans can be lost silently, which breaks PRD goal 4. Backup export and restore reuse the fflate dependency already in Phase 0, so the move is cheap. **Backup nudge (ST-4, pulled into Phase 1).** If `persist()` is denied or not yet requested, Settings says plainly that the browser may clear this plan. After 20 edits since the last backup, or 7 days with edits and no backup, the overview shows a dismissible banner: "Back up this plan" with one tap to export. Dismissing hides it until the next threshold.

### New requirement: SH-0, share image (P0)

**Entry points and output**
- **From a function:** produces one PNG for that function.
- **From the plan overview:** produces one overview PNG (one row per function) followed by one PNG per function, shared together as a single multi-file share. Where the browser does not support multi-file share, the overview PNG is shared alone and per-function images stay available from each function.
- Each image is portrait, about 1080 px wide. It shows the function name, date, and dress code, then the picked item per slot (photo or fallback tile, slot label, item title). Required slots with no pick are drawn as visible gaps. The footer shows the app name and URL.
- **Layout is provisional** pending /plan-design-review (Open Question 2): whether optional slots are omitted, whether a long function splits across images, and what each overview row shows.

**Share flow (iOS-safe)**
- Web Share with files needs a recent user tap, and rendering is async. So tapping Share opens the Share sheet and starts rendering right away; the sheet shows previews when ready. The **Send** button calls `navigator.share({ files })` directly in its own tap handler with the already-rendered blobs. No async work happens between that tap and the share call.
- Send is disabled, with a progress label ("Preparing 3 of 6"), until every image for the chosen output is rendered. If one image fails to render, the sheet shows that image as failed with Retry, and Send stays available for the images that succeeded. If every image fails, Send stays disabled and the sheet shows an error with Retry all.
- Decision order at Send: if `canShare` accepts all succeeded files, share them all; else if it accepts one file, share the overview PNG alone (per-function images stay available from each function), and if the overview PNG is the one that failed, Send stays disabled until its Retry succeeds; else use the phone or desktop fallback below.
- **Phone fallback (no file share):** a browser download on iOS Safari lands in the Files app, not Photos, so WhatsApp's photo picker cannot see it, and phones often block several downloads at once. On phones the fallback is a full-screen viewer that shows each rendered PNG one at a time with the hint "Press and hold the image, then Save to Photos". **Desktop fallback:** download a single PNG, or a zip when there are several.
- The Phase 1 gate includes a real-device check of the phone fallback on iOS Safari (forced by a test flag that reports `canShare` as false).
- If `share()` rejects with `AbortError` (user cancelled), do nothing. On `NotAllowedError` or any other error, show a toast with "Download instead" that triggers the download path.

**Rendering**
- Draw with the Canvas 2D API directly, not a DOM-to-image library, to keep the shell budget.
- Load the exact faces used (Newsreader, Instrument Sans) with `document.fonts.load()` before drawing; fonts are self-hosted with the app, and the Phase 3 service worker precaches them for offline use. Before Phase 3, an offline share may not have the fonts. If loading fails, offline or not, draw with the system serif and sans and still produce the image.
- Decode each photo with `createImageBitmap(blob, { resizeWidth, imageOrientation: "from-image" })` at the size it will be drawn, never at full resolution. Local photos are already resized to 1,600 px on capture per the PRD. A test fixture includes a large, EXIF-rotated photo. If a local blob fails to decode (for example HEIC on Android Chrome, or corruption), that slot draws the fallback tile and the image still renders; a corrupt-blob fixture covers this.
- Fonts: the canvas faces are subset to Latin plus the rupee sign, in the weights the image uses, and load lazily when the Share sheet opens. They are excluded from the 200 KB shell budget and tracked as a separate budget of 80 KB compressed in the bundle-size check.
- **Canvas tainting:** a remote image drawn without CORS permission blocks PNG export. Local blobs are always safe. Remote images load with `crossOrigin="anonymous"`; if that fails, the slot draws the fallback tile (title and domain), matching the broken-image state in the design.

**Share sheet in Phase 1:** the Share sheet offers the image only. "Send as link" appears in Phase 2 together with the shared view, so no link ever ships without a page to open it.

**Branding config:** the app name and footer URL come from build-time configuration (for example `VITE_APP_NAME`, `VITE_APP_URL`), so forks do not advertise the upstream site.

## Open Questions

1. **Link size budget with real URLs.** The PRD targets 6,000 characters for a typical plan (6 functions, 60 items) and warns past 8,000. Treat both numbers as provisional until eng review measures and tests tappability in WhatsApp on real devices. The budget test reports two sizes: the real Assignment fixture (about 20 items), and a 60-item, 6-function fixture made by spreading the real Assignment URLs across 6 functions with item titles and notes varied, so compression is not flattered by exact repeats. Product links (about 80 to 120 chars) and CDN image URLs (often 150+ chars) compress poorly, so the budget may require dropping remote image URLs past a limit, or sharing per function by default.
2. **Share image layout.** Women's preset has 13 slots and plans can have 8 functions. For /plan-design-review: optional slots in or out, when a function splits across images, what each overview row shows (thumbnails, completeness count, gaps), and the maximum functions per overview image.
3. **Analytics.** The PRD offers cookieless aggregate analytics or none. Success metrics below are written to be measurable either way; choosing none means relying on the moderated tests.
4. **Name and domain.** Owner decides before the Phase 1 gate. Until then the build uses placeholder config values, so this does not block development.
5. **Preview fetcher.** Still deferred. The share image reduces the pressure for one, because local screenshots become first-class.

## Success Criteria

- **Capture speed (premise 1):** in the Phase 1 plan-build test, saving a screenshot already on the phone takes at most 2 app taps: tap + and then tap Photo library. Taps inside the OS photo picker are not counted. The item is saved the moment the photo is picked: to the last used function and slot, which is preselected, or to the Inbox if there is none yet. Confirming or changing the slot is an optional extra step, as in the PRD's capture flow. This is the PRD's "capture takes at most two taps from any screen" check. Sharing a screenshot directly from another app arrives with the Phase 3 share target.
- **Time to first item:** median under 60 seconds, measured in moderated tests with at least 5 first-time users in Phase 1. If cookieless analytics ship, also tracked in aggregate.
- **Sharing:** in moderated tests, every tester can send a share image to WhatsApp unaided. If analytics ship, track the share rate of plans (target 30% sending an image or a link).
- **Data safety:** zero reported data loss, with persistent storage requested, backup and restore available, and the backup nudge active from Phase 1.
- Every P0 ID, including SH-0, has at least one test that names it. Phase gates pass on real devices, not just emulators.

### DR15: Overlay focus and screen readers (Pass 6, D15 = A)

### DR20: Long names on share images (Pass 7, D20 = A)
